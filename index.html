Introduction

About Dr Chan

Dr Chan’s work spans family medicine, musculoskeletal medicine, medical education, clinical teaching, research, and AI applications for health professionals.

His qualifications include degrees from the University of Hong Kong and the University of Sydney, alongside professional qualifications from colleges in Hong Kong, Australia, the United Kingdom, and Ireland. He retired from clinical work in 2021 and continues to teach and research into AI for doctors.

Profile

Professional Focus

Three connected areas of professional service, teaching, and development.

Family Medicine

Dr Chan has contributed to the development of family medicine through clinical supervision, examinations, quality assurance, postgraduate education, and teaching across Hong Kong and the region.

Service to The Hong Kong College of Family Physicians

  • College Supervisor and Mentor, 1986–2024.
  • Member of the organizing committee for the WONCA Asia Pacific Regional Conference 1987.
  • Censor, 2006–2009.
  • Examiner for the conjoint RACGP/HKCGP fellowship examination, 1988–2024.
  • Chairman of the Quality Assurance and Accreditation Committee, 1998–2004.
  • Assessor for the FHKAM (FM) Exit Examination from 1998 onward.

Regional education and development

Dr Chan’s contributions include participation in establishing the RACGP/HKCFP conjoint examination; introducing CPD, a continuing professional development component to the College’s quality assurance programme; and developing reciprocal recognition of that programme with the The Royal Australian College of General Practitioners.

He also established an Assessment Enhancement course for family medicine trainees, edited a textbook Problem-Based Approach to Family Medicine, and introduced a Diploma in Family Medicine course to Macau. He has participated in family medicine education in Hong Kong, Macau, and mainland China, including teaching doctors in Shenzhen and Beijing.

Musculoskeletal Medicine

Dr Chan developed an interest in musculoskeletal problems while practising family medicine in primary care. He completed a Graduate Diploma in Physical Medicine (Musculoskeletal) at the University of Sydney in 1998, then established a clinic in Tsuen Wan where he introduced the approaches he had studied. Over the following years, he continued his training through courses, workshops, and conferences in Europe, North America, Australia, and New Zealand. In 2001, he completed a Master’s degree in Physical Medicine (Musculoskeletal) at the University of Sydney.

In 2004, the Medical Council of Hong Kong recognized his Sydney master’s qualification as a quotable degree. Dr Chan subsequently focused on sharing his musculoskeletal medicine knowledge and skills among colleagues. In July 2005, he founded the Hong Kong Institute of Musculoskeletal Medicine (HKIMM) and served as its founding president.

Training and professional development

HKIMM organized regular lectures and workshops to develop education in musculoskeletal medicine. In 2010, it introduced the Certificate Course of Practical Musculoskeletal Medicine (CPMM), with 18 doctors in its first cohort. Some participants later passed the Institute’s fellowship examination and became examiners themselves.

In 2011, HKIMM was invited to join the International Federation of Manual/Musculoskeletal Medicine (FIMM). The CPMM course continued through 2024 and was upgraded to a diploma course in 2025.

Fellowship

HKIMM also established a fellowship examination to assess knowledge and clinical skills and to encourage continuing professional development. In a recent fellowship examination held in August 2026, 13 doctors from Hong Kong, Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore passed the examination and became fellow of the Institute.

AI & Medical Education

Following his retirement from clinical work in 2021, Dr Chan continued teaching family medicine and musculoskeletal medicine to medical students and postgraduate trainees.

Dr Chan has had a longstanding interest in programming. He wrote DOS-based clinic software using Clipper Summer ’87 in 1987 and later developed a Windows-based Clinic Automation System by Healthindices (CASH), using Borland Delphi 7 in 2002.

As artificial intelligence developed rapidly in the early 2020s, he began exploring how doctors could embrace AI to improve productivity while protecting patient confidentiality.

His current educational interests include organizing AI workshops for health professionals and developing a locally hosted AI tool intended for doctors.
